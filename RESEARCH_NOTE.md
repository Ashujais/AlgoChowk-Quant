# Research Note — NIFTY decline and recovery

**Hypothesis.** After a close-to-close NIFTY 50 fall of at least 2%, a long position entered at the next open and exited at the fifth subsequent session's close has positive net return and exceeds an unconditional same-period NIFTY forward-return baseline. The 2% cutoff represents a conspicuous daily decline; five sessions approximates one trading week. These are fixed research choices, not fitted returns.

**Design.** Source: Yahoo Finance `^NSEI` daily OHLC snapshot, 2013-01-02–2026-07-07. Signal is observable only after event-day close. Entry: next source-observed session open; exit: fifth session close (entry session is day 1). Recovery means net return above zero; excess recovery means net return above the unconditional mean. Both are assessed. Per-side cost 5 bps plus slippage 5 bps; net = exit × (1−0.001) / [entry × (1+0.001)] − 1. The price index is a proxy, not a tradable instrument. Selected events cannot overlap: a new signal must occur after the prior exit. Raw and selected counts are reported. Missing OHLC bars and all candidate windows crossing them are excluded; unusual valid moves remain. Development opportunities exit before 2022-01-01; out-of-sample (OOS) signals start on/after that date. No period crosses the split.

**Evidence.** Development: 62 raw signals, 44 non-overlapping events, 2,117 eligible baseline dates. Event mean net return **−0.447%**, median **+0.421%**, standard deviation **3.921%**, win rate **52.3%**. The 95% seeded event bootstrap CI for mean net return is **[−1.610%, +0.627%]**; one-sided p for positive mean is **0.785**. Baseline mean net return is **−0.054%**, median **+0.045%**, standard deviation **2.333%**, win rate **50.9%**. Event-minus-baseline mean is **−0.393 percentage points**, 95% bootstrap CI **[−1.594, +0.685] percentage points**, one-sided p for positive excess **0.749**. Mean exposes tail losses; median, win rate, spread, and percentiles in the CSV describe the distribution.

**Robustness and OOS.** Development thresholds −1.5%, −2%, −2.5% and horizons 1, 3, 5, 10 sessions give mixed means; every net excess interval in this grid includes zero. At the primary rule, allowing overlap changes the mean from −0.447% to −0.343%; zero versus 20 bps round-trip per-side cost/slippage changes it from −0.248% to −0.646%. OOS: 19 raw signals, 14 selected, 1,083 baseline dates. Event mean net **+0.627%**, median **−0.347%**, win rate **42.9%**; baseline mean net **−0.088%**. Mean excess **+0.715 percentage points**, 95% CI **[−0.858, +2.396]**, p **0.188**. The sign changed, but neither interval excludes zero. Multiple thresholds and horizons invite data snooping; no attractive row is adopted post hoc.

**Falsification and conclusion.** The original trading hypothesis would require positive net excess that survives the development and OOS uncertainty bounds, costs, overlap treatment, and plausible vendor corrections. It fails the predeclared evidence gate now: development net mean and excess are negative, and OOS estimates are too imprecise. Regime breakdown: 27 selected 2013–2019 events averaged +0.035% net, while 17 events in 2020–2021 averaged −1.214%; this shift challenges stationarity. Crisis clustering, only 44/14 independent selected observations, secondary-vendor gaps, and an index that cannot be filled at its displayed open further limit inference. The conditional backtest was not run. The result is **insufficient evidence for a reliable tradable rebound**, not proof that recoveries never occur.

*Method:* seeded percentile bootstrap resamples selected non-overlapping events; baseline means use circular 20-candidate-day blocks. One-sided centered bootstrap p-values test mean > 0 and event-minus-baseline mean > 0 at 5%. Intervals approximate uncertainty and do not prove causality or execution feasibility.
